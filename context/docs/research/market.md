### 1.3 Durbin Amendment and Regulation II (debit interchange)

- What changed: on October 26, 2026 (CORRECTED in pass 2; the first pass wrote "October 27" from the ABA article, but the opinion itself reads "Filed: October 26, 2026") the Eighth Circuit in Corner Post Inc. v. Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System unanimously affirmed the North Dakota district court's decision to vacate Regulation II. The panel found the Fed "exceeded its statutory authority by failing to distinguish between incremental costs and fixed costs" and "remanded the matter for the agency to develop a fee structure consistent with Congress's instructions." [secondary, ABA Banking Journal, published Nov. 3, 2026] (Source: https://bankingjournal.aba.com/2026/11/eighth-circuit-affirms-vacatur-of-regulation-ii/, checked 2026-11-14)
- Conflicting appeal: a Kentucky federal court sided with the Fed in Linney's Pizza; the Sixth Circuit "has yet to issue its decision." (Source: https://bankingjournal.aba.com/2026/11/eighth-circuit-affirms-vacatur-of-regulation-ii/, checked 2026-11-14)
- Primary opinion (pass 2): Eighth Circuit No. 25-3000, Corner Post, Inc. v. Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System, "Submitted: May 13, 2026," "Filed: October 26, 2026," before Judges L.R. Smith, Benton and Stras (Stras writing). Opening: "Banks charge fees every time a customer swipes a debit card. The question here is how high they can be. Congress said it depends on the 'incremental cost' of the transaction, 15 U.S.C. § 1693o-2(a)(4)(B)(i), so we affirm the decision vacating the 21-cent cap set by the Federal Reserve." Disposition: "We accordingly affirm the judgment of the district court, which vacates the current 21-cent cap ... and remand for the agency to come up with a fee structure that reflects the statute Congress wrote." [primary, opinion PDF read as text] (Source: https://ecf.ca8.uscourts.gov/opndir/26/10/253000P.pdf, checked 2026-11-14)
- Stay and mandate (pass 2): the opinion says the district court "ordered vacatur but stayed its decision pending the outcome of this appeal." Footnote 5: "Our decision 'only ... become[s] effective' when we issue our mandate ... which will happen seven days after the deadline to file a petition for rehearing or an order denying one. See Fed. R. App. P. 41(b); see also id. 40(d)(1) (giving parties 45 days to seek rehearing in cases involving federal agencies)." [primary] (Source: https://ecf.ca8.uscourts.gov/opndir/26/10/253000P.pdf, checked 2026-11-14)
- INFERRED arithmetic: 45 days from the October 26, 2026 filing date is December 10, 2026, and seven days after that is December 17, 2026. This is my calculation from the opinion's footnote, not a date stated by the court.
- Background: the 2011 rule set a cap of 21 cents per transaction plus 0.05% of the transaction value; the district court (Aug. 6, 2025) vacated the rule but stayed the vacatur pending appeal. [the stay is now primary, from the opinion; the 0.05% and August 6, 2025 date remain snippet only] (Source: https://www.consumerfinancemonitor.com/2026/10/28/eighth-circuit-invalidates-federal-reserves-21-cent-debit-card-interchange-fee-cap-holding-that-the-durbin-amendment-requires-a-transaction-specific-cost-analysis/, checked 2026-11-14; page returned 403)
- Sixth Circuit (pass 2): a search summary says the Sixth Circuit heard argument on October 22, 2026 in the Linney's Pizza appeal, so a circuit split is possible. [snippet only; not verified on the page] (Source: https://www.globalfinregblog.com/2026/11/eighth-circuit-affirms-vacatur-of-federal-reserve-debit-interchange-fee-cap/, checked 2026-11-14; crawl returned almost no text)
- Not found: whether rehearing en banc or certiorari has been sought, and whether the mandate has issued (no report located as of 2026-11-14); the Federal Reserve's own response (federalreserve.gov Regulation II page returned no 2026 content).
