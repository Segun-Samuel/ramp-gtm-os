- What changed: Judge Brian Cogan (Eastern District of New York, Brooklyn) granted preliminary approval to a revised Visa and Mastercard settlement with merchants on June 9, 2026. [secondary] The Payments Dive story is dated June 9, 2026 and reports that "Judge Brian Cogan granted preliminary approval." (Source: https://www.paymentsdive.com/news/court-approves-visa-mastercard-settlement/822440/, checked 2026-11-14)

- Rate terms: credit interchange rates trimmed by "10 basis points" applied at varying rates for five years, and the settlement would "impose a 1.25% rate for standard consumer cards for eight years." [secondary] (Source: https://www.paymentsdive.com/news/court-approves-visa-mastercard-settlement/822440/, checked 2026-11-14)
- Commercial and premium cards: the settlement would let merchants decline some higher-cost premium and commercial credit cards, a departure from the networks' honor-all-cards rule. [secondary] (Source: https://www.paymentsdive.com/news/court-approves-visa-mastercard-settlement/822440/, checked 2026-11-14)
- Surcharge: "The settlement allows a merchant to levy a surcharge up to 3% to cover their costs for premium and commercial cards." Also "The pact also allows merchants to reject certain pricey, high-end credit cards." [secondary] (Source: https://www.paymentsdive.com/news/why-pricey-credit-cards-may-soon-cost-more/825609/, checked 2026-11-14)
- Date conflict resolved: some search results give April 27, 2026 as the preliminary approval date. [secondary] (Source: https://www.paymentsdive.com/news/why-pricey-credit-cards-may-soon-cost-more/825609/, checked 2026-11-14)

### 1.3 Durbin Amendment and Regulation II (debit interchange)

- What changed: on October 27, 2026 the Eighth Circuit in Corner Post Inc. v. Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System unanimously affirmed the North Dakota district court's decision to vacate Regulation II. The panel found the Fed "exceeded its statutory authority by failing to distinguish between incremental costs and fixed costs" and "remanded the matter for the agency to develop a fee structure consistent with Congress's instructions." [secondary, ABA Banking Journal, published Nov. 3, 2026] (Source: https://bankingjournal.aba.com/2026/11/eighth-circuit-affirms-vacatur-of-regulation-ii/, checked 2026-11-14)
- Conflicting appeal: a Kentucky federal court sided with the Fed in Linney's Pizza; the Sixth Circuit "has yet to issue its decision." (Source: https://bankingjournal.aba.com/2026/11/eighth-circuit-affirms-vacatur-of-regulation-ii/, checked 2026-11-14)
- Background: the 2011 rule set a cap of 21 cents per transaction plus 0.05% of the transaction value; the district court (Aug. 6, 2025) vacated the rule but stayed the vacatur pending appeal. [snippet only] (Source: https://www.consumerfinancemonitor.com/2026/10/28/eighth-circuit-invalidates-federal-reserves-21-cent-debit-card-interchange-fee-cap-holding-that-the-durbin-amendment-requires-a-transaction-specific-cost-analysis/, checked 2026-11-14; page returned 403)
- Not found: whether rehearing en banc or certiorari has been sought, and whether the stay on vacatur has been lifted.
- Who is affected: debit card issuers and merchants. In force: vacatur affirmed; effect of the stay not confirmed.
- INFERRED: Regulation II concerns debit interchange. Ramp's core product is a corporate card, so a direct effect on Ramp's buyers is unclear. No cited source addresses this.

## 2. Paper check phase-out and check fraud

### 2.1 Executive Order 14247

- What changed: Executive Order 14247, "Modernizing Payments To and From America's Bank Account," dated March 25, 2025. Purpose: "The continued use of paper-based payments by the Federal Government, including checks and money orders," "imposes unnecessary costs; delays; and risks of fraud, lost payments, theft, and inefficiencies." [primary] (Source: https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/03/modernizing-payments-to-and-from-americas-bank-account/, checked 2026-11-14)
- Deadline: "Effective September 30, 2025, and to the extent permitted by law," "the Secretary of the Treasury shall cease issuing paper checks for all Federal disbursements." (Source: https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/03/modernizing-payments-to-and-from-americas-bank-account/, checked 2026-11-14)
- Exceptions: Treasury reviews procedures for "limited exceptions"; those qualifying "shall be provided alternative payment options." Payments to the government: "As soon as practicable, and to the extent permitted by law," "all payments made to the Federal Government shall be processed electronically." (Source: https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/03/modernizing-payments-to-and-from-americas-bank-account/, checked 2026-11-14)
- Treasury implementation: Treasury issued a news release on August 14, 2025 saying it would stop paper checks for most federal benefit items on September 30, 2025; listed possible exceptions include emergency payments where electronic disbursement would cause undue hardship and national security or law enforcement activities. [secondary] (Source: https://www.bdo.com/insights/tax/treasury-to-stop-issuing-paper-checks-on-september-30-2025, checked 2026-11-14)
- White House fact-sheet statistics: Treasury checks are "16 times more likely to be reported lost or stolen" than an electronic funds transfer ("Historically"); maintaining paper-record infrastructure "cost the American taxpayer over $657 million in fiscal year 2024 alone"; banks filed about 680,000 reports of check fraud in 2022, nearly double 2021. [primary] (Source: https://www.whitehouse.gov/fact-sheets/2025/03/fact-sheet-president-donald-j-trump-modernizes-payments-to-and-from-americas-bank-account, checked 2026-11-14)
- Scope: the order governs federal disbursements and payments to the federal government. Direct effect on private B2B payments: Not found in any source. A private-sector check mandate does not appear in the order text I read.
- Who is affected: federal payees (vendors paid by federal agencies, benefit recipients) and anyone paying the federal government. In force: Yes (September 30, 2025 passed).
