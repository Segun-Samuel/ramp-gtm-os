# Ramp: Buying Signals and Trigger Events (raw)

Researched: 2026-11-14

Scope: Raw public-source evidence of what makes a company need Ramp now, i.e. observable buying signals and trigger events a GTM team could detect from public data (hiring, funding, new leaders, expansion, deadlines, tool changes). Evidence only: no ranking, scoring, conclusions or recommendations. Company, customers, competitors, market and voice are covered by other files.

Method note:
- Web pages were read through a summarising fetch tool. Text inside quotation marks is only what the tool returned as verbatim; everything else is a paraphrase. Where a tool flagged that a page was truncated, that is noted.
- Ramp job postings were read from Ramp's public Ashby job-board JSON feed (no login), so job-description quotes are exact (curly apostrophes normalised to straight ones).
- Labels: STATED = the source says the trigger directly. INFERRED = my own link between a signal and Ramp need. FACT = a dated fact with no trigger claim. UNVERIFIED = seen only in a search-result summary, or the page itself could not be fetched.
- Pages that returned HTTP 403 or 404 to the fetch tool are listed in "Gaps and not found". Nothing was logged into, no paywall bypassed, no browser automation used.
- Ramp is the author of most "switch" and "when to adopt" pages below, so the framing is vendor marketing. Third-party and vendor-affiliated sources are labelled as such.

## 1. Ramp's own stated triggers (website, blog, switching pages)

### 1.1 Blog: when to switch cards or tools

- STATED (Ramp blog, "When should you switch to a different business credit card?", published January 22, 2026). Listed switching triggers, quoted as returned by the fetch tool:
  - "Most businesses switch cards when spending patterns shift, growth accelerates"
  - "If you're spending $50,000 per month but your card caps rewards at a lower amount"
  - "making multiple payments per month just to free up available credit"
  - "Your card issuer has been acquired or is undergoing major changes" (a heading), followed by "it's worth evaluating your options before the transition—not after"
  - "If you can't set spending limits per employee card"
  - "As your team grows, these features shift from nice-to-have to essential"
  - "If you started as a sole proprietorship and have since incorporated as an LLC or S-corp"
  - "If your card's value proposition has eroded since you signed up, it may be time to explore alternatives"
  - Size contrast, the only one given: "A card that worked for a $200,000 startup won't serve a $2 million company with a distributed team." The page sets no formal size threshold. (Source: https://ramp.com/blog/when-to-switch-business-credit-cards, checked 2026-11-14)
- STATED (Ramp blog, "When to apply for a business credit card: Signs you're ready", dated October 19, 2026):
  - "Apply for a business credit card as soon as you have regular business expenses you want off your personal card."
  - Once even one employee buys for the business, individual employee cards work better than reimbursements or shared card numbers (paraphrase).
  - Corporate cards generally fit funded startups or companies with a large US bank balance, "often $20,000 or more."
  - The page sets no explicit threshold for moving to a spend management platform. (Source: https://ramp.com/blog/when-to-get-a-business-credit-card, checked 2026-11-14)

  - "Every controller's performance is measured by how fast and how accurately the books close." (Source: https://ramp.com/blog/enterprise-spend-management-for-controllers, checked 2026-11-14)
