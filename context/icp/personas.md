# Ramp buyer personas

Status: draft v1, 2026-11-14. Built only from `context/docs/research/` (all sources checked 2026-11-14). Read with [icp.md](icp.md).

How to read:
- Source codes: `com` = company.md, `cus` = customers.md, `cmp` = competitors.md, `sig` = signals.md, `voi` = voice.md, then the section number. The URL sits next to the fact in that file.
- Pains are verbatim customer quotes. Almost all come from Ramp-published case studies, so they are vendor-selected. Objections come from review sites and a competitor's (Brex's) page.
- INFERRED = my reading, not stated by a source.

v1 target segment is US Mid-Market (icp.md section 1). There the economic buyer is the CFO or VP Finance; the founder or CEO lines below apply to SMB only.

## Buying group at a glance

| Persona | Role in the deal | Typical titles | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Champion | Feels the pain, runs the evaluation | Controller, VP Controller, Assistant Controller, VP of Accounting, Director of Accounting | cus 3, sig 2.5 |
| Economic buyer | Owns budget and signs | CFO, VP Finance, Head of Finance; founder or CEO at SMB | sig 4.1, cus 7 |
| Influencer | Does the daily work, decides if it sticks | AP Manager, Accounting Manager, Senior or Staff Accountant | cus 7, sig 2.6 |

## 1. Champion: the Controller

**Job titles seen in the research**
- Controller (Perplexity, Construction One, Apprentice.io, Seed, Barry's, REVA)
- VP Controller (Zola, Virgin Voyages); Corporate Controller (SAMGI)
- Assistant Controller (MAGNA-TILES, Notion); Deputy Controller (Fi)
- VP of Accounting and Controller (Rarebreed); VP of Accounting (Brandt)
- Director of Accounting (Opendoor, Evans Hotels); Chief Accounting Officer (Notion)

[cus 2, cus 3, cus 7, sig 2.5]

**What they're measured on**
- Close speed and accuracy: "Every controller's performance is measured by how fast and how accurately the books close." [sig 1.1]
- Audit readiness: "Controllers want spend management that generates audit documentation as a byproduct of normal operations." [sig 8]
- Scaling without new accounting hires: "With Ramp, we haven't had to add accounting headcount to keep up with growth." (Melissa M., VP of Accounting, Brandt) [cus 3.34]
- Note: the first two lines are Ramp's framing in its own guides, not a survey of controllers.

**Top three pains, in their words**
1. "We were collecting and reviewing receipts manually, and so there were a lot of things we couldn't review in time" (Van Dinh, Deputy Controller, Fi) [sig 2.3]
2. "Our AP person would go into 75 different Google docs, make one giant report, turn it into an Excel spreadsheet, and import it into our software," (Chris Moberger, Controller, Construction One) [sig 2.5]
3. "I didn't like it at all -- it's horrible internal control," (Tim Borse, Assistant Controller, MAGNA-TILES) [sig 2.5]

**What makes them start looking**
- They join and inherit a weak process. Apprentice.io's Controller: "I started thinking: is how I'm operating my accounting outdated?" [sig 2.5]
- An audit is coming: "Nine times out of 10, you're hired because your company is facing its first audit." ABB Optical's quarterly audits took about two months. [sig 4.2, sig 1.6]
- Close stretches past 10 days (Mix Talent, WizeHire, Walther Farms, Zola). [sig 1.6, sig 2.7]
- The ERP sync needs constant fixing, or an ERP migration is planned (8VC, Brandt). [sig 2.6]
- The card programme can't keep up: "We had a $30,000 monthly limit for a $40M revenue business," (Eric Chabot, VP of Accounting and Controller, Rarebreed) [cus 7]

**Objections**

| Objection | In their words (who) | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Will it sync cleanly with our ERP? | "The accounting side of Ramp is glitchy and difficult to use" (Manager, Finance and Accounting, 51-200 employees). A Controller's Gartner review is titled "Receipt matching and cost-free usage stand out, but integration is difficult" | cus 6.6, cus 6.3 |
| I don't trust AI to code my books | "Finance professionals are by nature more skeptical of AI," (Lauren Feeney, Controller, Perplexity). "The receipt automatic coding is becoming a huge pain trying to figure out how to make it stop auto clearing and auto coding." (Gartner reviewer) | cus 3.2, cus 6.3 |
| Reporting won't meet my needs | "The reports options could be more customizable, and the CSV exports should be easier to clean up and save." (Analyst, Finance and Accounting, 11-50 employees) | cus 6.6 |
| Changes land without warning | "Ramp frequently releases new features but doesn't notify admins of the new functionalities, often creating confusion when changes have been rolled out and are found accidentally." (Controller, 51-200 employees) | cus 6.6 |
| A migration will disrupt the close | INFERRED from Ramp's own pre-emption: "Most finance software takes months to implement. Ramp only takes days." | sig 1.3 |

## 2. Economic buyer: CFO or VP Finance (founder or CEO at SMB)

**Job titles seen in the research**
- CFO (Denver Broncos, Walther Farms, FirstBlood, Dragonfly Pond Works, JDC Power Systems, Hingham)
- VP of Finance (New Way Landscape, Skin Pharm); SVP Finance (Studs)
- Head of Finance (Attio); Head of Strategy and Finance (UpEquity)
- At SMB: Founder and CEO (Sandboxx), CEO (Eight Sleep, WizeHire, Piñata). Ramp's SMB reps sell to "founders, CEOs, and finance leaders".

[cus 2, cus 3, cus 7, sig 2.1, sig 4.1]

**What they're measured on**
- Speed and insight without more headcount: Ramp says finance teams must "move faster and deliver deeper insights" without adding headcount. [sig 1.1]
- Control and board-ready numbers: Ramp's CFO guide says uncontrolled spend brings slower closes, stale board data and audit gaps (paraphrase). [sig 1.1]
- Capital efficiency: "What we needed was capital efficiency, spend monitoring, and overarching insight and awareness of our spend." (Matthew Ferguson, Controller, TaskHuman) [sig 2.1]
- Absorbing acquisitions: Ramp asks whether a CFO's systems can take the next acquisition "without adding headcount." [sig 6.1]
- Note: Ramp's framing; no CFO survey in the research.

**Top three pains, in their words**
1. "Every one of those tools was a reasonable choice on its own but they didn't talk to each other." (Justin Webster, CFO, Denver Broncos) [cus 7]
2. "It was like finding a needle in a haystack each month," (Josh Reeves, CFO, Walther Farms, on a close of about 20 days) [sig 2.7]
3. "It was a terrible world where everyone was sharing one AmEx card" (Sid Upadhyay, CEO, WizeHire) [cus 7]

**What makes them start looking**
- Growth forces a choice. Brandt's story: "add people, or redesign the process." [sig 2.1]
- Hypergrowth after a raise (TaskHuman, Series B; narration only). [sig 2.2]
- An acquisition adds entities (Align ENTA). [sig 2.9]
- A contract comes up for renewal (Betterment). [sig 2.6]
- The incumbent changes owner. Ramp on Brex: "teams who thought they were getting a nimble fintech and wound up with a legacy bank." [sig 1.6]
- Tool costs keep rising: "Bill.com's licensing fees kept climbing year over year as transaction volume grew." (Mix Talent) [sig 1.6]
- Fraud exposure or a card limit that is too low (WizeHire, Rarebreed). [sig 2.8, cus 7]

**Objections**

| Objection | In their words (who) | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Price, and price changes | "They are changing their pricing and I'm not sure how much that will keep increasing. It's hard to plan them as a long term solution when that's happening." (Employee, Finance and Accounting, 201-500 employees). Capterra: "very expensive relative to others". The Plus platform fee is not published | cus 6.6, cus 6.2, com 3 |
| Not deep enough | "In my opinion, it's too half baked for going all in on expense." and "visibility and reporting are severely lacking despite claims of AI and intelligence." (CFO, 201-500 employees, 2/10, May 2024) | cus 6.6 |
| Card tool, not spend tool | "Ramp felt designed around card management but not really spend management" (Numeral, on Brex's page; competitor marketing) | sig 2.10 |
| Support and vendor risk | Trustpilot 2.7 out of 5: "Customer service is lost with Ramp." Brex's page (competitor marketing): "Ramp customer service left them waiting weeks" | cus 6.4, sig 2.10 |
| We'd lose our rewards | INFERRED from Ramp's rebuttal on its Amex page: "customers often save more with Ramp than they ever did trying to game Amex rewards". Ramp's Airwallex FAQ: "Airwallex advertises up to 2% cashback. Ramp is up to 1.5%." | cmp 3 |

## 3. Influencer: AP and accounting operations

**Job titles seen in the research**
- AP Manager (Pair Eyewear); accounts payable (Denver Broncos)
- Accounting Manager (Skin Pharm); Sr. Accounting Manager (Heyday)
- Senior Accountant (Betterment); Staff Accountant (Brandt); Accounting Associate (Snapdocs); finance coordinator (Crossings Community Church)
- Adjacent voices: Director of FP&A (Align ENTA), Head of Business Operations (Foursquare), Senior Payroll, T/E Manager (Anduril), Director of Compliance and Privacy Officer (ABB Optical), Director of Enterprise Risk Management (Prosper)

[cus 3, cus 7, sig 1.6, sig 2.6, sig 2.9, voi 1]

**What they're measured on**
- Not found as a stated KPI.
- Proxies the research uses (INFERRED as their measures): hours of manual work (Crossings' finance coordinator spent "40 hours a week just on invoice and expense tasks", narration); receipt compliance (Piñata had "nearly 40% of transactions missing receipts"); reconciliation time (Snapdocs 5 to 6 hours a month); employee adoption ("That's what drives 100% adoption.", Foursquare). [sig 1.6, voi 1]
- Ramp sells to these measures: "Up to 7x fewer clicks to process a bill vs Bill.com" and "2.4x faster invoice processing than legacy software". [voi 3.3]

**Top three pains, in their words**
1. "We would have to wait until the statement closed at the end of the month to download all transactions" (Amy F., Staff Accountant, Brandt) [cus 7]
2. "Previously with Brex and Expensify, we had to do a lot of manual syncing on our end." (Fahem Islam, Accounting Associate, Snapdocs) [cus 7]
3. "Our previous system lacked efficiency. Everything automatically went to our parent subsidiary" (Marianne Hawes, Senior Accountant, Betterment) [sig 2.6]

**What makes them start looking**
- They rarely start it (INFERRED: in the stories a controller, CFO or CEO leads; AP voices appear after the switch).
- They supply the evidence that starts it: approvals that stall ("On Concur, once everything's inputted for month end, it takes a week or two to chase down approvers," Gaby Edmead, J/PR, title not given) and tool sprawl across locations ("10 locations might mean 10 different spend management instances." Shawn Gordon, Sr. Accounting Manager, Heyday). [sig 1.3, sig 2.9]

**Objections**

| Objection | In their words (who) | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Setup is confusing | "The permissions and roles set up process is confusing." (Gartner reviewer) | cus 6.3 |
| Daily friction | "I don't like the sign-in process; sometimes it can really give you a hard time." (G2 review via AWS mirror). "Mobile app can be buggy sometimes" (COO, 1-10 employees). G2 theme: rigid approval workflows | cus 6.1, cus 6.6 |
| We lose features | "Ramp Plus took away a good amount of functionality that was available to us before it was introduced" (Manager, Finance and Accounting, 51-200 employees) | cus 6.6 |
| Our other systems won't connect | "It does not integrate with Lightspeed so you need to use a universal csv process which is difficult to upload the Lightspeed." (Gartner reviewer) | cus 6.3 |
| Vendors will complain | A vendor paid through Ramp on Trustpilot: "Our experience with Ramp is that they do not pay on our invoices in a timely manner." | cus 6.4 |
